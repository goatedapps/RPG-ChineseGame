1

I got ready in Scholar Village

After the introduction, Grandma Wang welcomed me and gave me the Spirit Brush handle. A Fogling appeared, so my first fight taught me how to free a Word Spirit.

I opened the Spirit Book from the left menu. It separates words by lesson. A new card is Bronze; answering its Meaning, Pinyin, Hanzi, Usage and Writing questions fills its circles. Silver and Gold are useful bonuses, but Bronze is enough for the boss requirement.

  • Reading Hall: take a normal passage, then answer the villagers marked ?. An “I don’t know” review helps, but only a correct answer clears the question. Finishing the passage awards the Cave Lantern and saves its scroll.
  • School: practise words from Lessons 1–3, including dictation. I could repeat lessons for XP, though only the first three daily School runs paid coins.
  • Inn: when HP was low, I answered three questions to rest. The Inn uses this region’s vocabulary.
  • Shop: I bought a Rice Ball for healing and considered a 10-coin Spirit Bait before searching for a specific missing word.

My village compass

The north exit leads to Mistwood Road. School and Inn are in the northern half of town; Reading Hall and Shop are in the southern half. The Storyteller is near the eastern path.

The Adventure menu and Next step message helped me remember my goal, but they did not lock me to one route.

2

I explored the foggy road

I entered from the Scholar Village side at the lower left. The fog cleared as I walked. I could step on forest floor, flowers and old road, but not through thickets or ponds; those blocked areas revealed themselves when I came beside them.

3

I used my book, bag and equipment

Why battle? A creature holds a Word Spirit. Winning can add its card to my Spirit Book and gives XP and coins. I needed enough different Bronze cards to challenge the boss; repeatedly fighting only low-level creatures was a slow way to gain levels.

In a fight, “weak spot” means that attack hurts this creature more. “Can fill an empty skill circle” means a correct answer can teach that Spirit a skill it has not yet mastered. The hint changes when that circle is filled. I could choose another attack when it suited me.

When a missing word would not appear, the Shop’s Spirit Bait let me choose the exact word from that lesson for my next encounter there. The shop marks missing and already-collected words differently.

What I kept in the Bag

Consumables, materials, key items and scrolls live together here. The Cave Lantern is the key item from Reading Hall. The Dawn Stroke comes after the boss.

Hero Status shows my stats and equipped brush, charm and hat. Changing equipment is separate from using a consumable.

Rice Ball · 25 coins. Heals in a fight; useful when I was close to losing.

Power Tea / Guardian Talisman · 45 coins each. Boost attacks or soften counterattacks during a battle.

Red Cap · 40 coins. Shop equipment that adds 3 maximum HP.

Scholar’s Lantern · 40 coins. For 40 forest steps, avoids spirits I already raised to Silver or Gold.

Forest Repellent · 30 coins. Stops encounters for 40 forest steps; useful for travel, not collecting.

Smoke Ball · 20 coins. A safer way out when a fight is too much; ordinary Run safely is chance-based.

Optional in Scholar Village: goals and rewards
  • Xiaoqiang: collect 贵重 and 探险, find his treasure box in Camping Forest, then raise 狼吞虎咽 to Bronze. The request names the Jade Brush recipe; the game grants three Mist Drops and two Ink Beads toward crafting it.
  • Mr Lin: raise 模糊 and 眼圈 to Bronze, defeat three Twin Shades, and write 距离 successfully. He gives Grandma’s Lantern as a key keepsake.
  • Chef Mei: raise 调味料 and 材料 to Bronze, defeat two Ink Imps, and answer three Lesson 3 tingxie words correctly. The request names the Mooncake recipe; the game grants one Mooncake in the Bag.

The Storyteller gives 10 coins the first time I finish each regional story. The Restoration Board asks me to raise every card in a themed set to Silver or Gold; each offered set repairs part of town and adds a room decoration without consuming cards. The sets here are Campfire, Kitchen, Healthy Eyes, Kind Words and Team Spirit.

The Daily Board asks for three daily tasks; the chest gives 30 coins, one Rice Ball, one Mist Drop and one Echo Feather. At the Craft Table, the Jade Brush takes 20 coins plus Xiaoqiang’s three Mist Drops and two Ink Beads; an Echo Bell or Mirror Charm takes 20 coins and four matching creature drops.

After the boss, I could visit the reformed Muddle King’s Mistake Museum. The restored Dawn Stroke also opens the Hidden Grove for a special scroll and 30 coins. None of these detours blocks the onward gate.

4

I faced the Muddle King and reached the gate

The Muddle Pavilion became my next goal once I had the Cave Lantern and at least 44 different Bronze-or-better cards from this region. That is 54 regional words in total for this curriculum. Silver and Gold were not required.

Inside the Pavilion, the Muddle King fought back. The challenge mixed standalone word questions with writing from memory; it did not expect me to answer from a hidden passage. I brought healing and a Guardian Talisman, and I could open my Bag during the boss fight. Winning restored the Spirit Brush’s Dawn Stroke.

My last test before Harvest Crossing

At the road gate, I had to write 15 collected regional words from memory and get 13 correct under the default settings. The prompt reads the word aloud, shows meaning, pinyin and a blanked sentence, but not the target Chinese characters. I could practise at School and return to try again. After I passed, the gate opened and I arrived at Harvest Crossing.
